## 1. PURPOSE

This policy establishes a fair, accessible, consistent and auditable process for receiving, acknowledging, investigating, escalating, resolving and learning from complaints and disputes.

The organisation intends to:
- make it reasonably straightforward to raise a complaint;
- treat complainants respectfully and without retaliation;
- assess complaints impartially and proportionately;
- preserve relevant evidence and protect personal information;
- provide clear progress updates and explain outcomes;
- identify systemic failures and corrective actions;
- preserve customers' statutory rights and access to competent external bodies.

## 2. SCOPE

This policy applies to complaints from customers, prospective customers, users, business clients and other persons affected by an in-scope product or service.

It may cover:
- product or service quality, availability or performance;
- billing, invoices, subscriptions, cancellations and refunds;
- payment processing, payout reporting and reconciliation;
- account access, identity verification and customer support;
- delivery, fulfilment, warranties and representations;
- privacy, personal-information handling and data-subject requests;
- accessibility, discrimination, conduct or customer treatment;
- suspected fraud, security incidents or unauthorised activity;
- contractual disputes and other service-related grievances.

Employee grievances, supplier disputes, employment matters, formal litigation, law-enforcement requests and regulatory investigations must be routed to their designated specialist procedures. A complaint must not be rejected merely because it also triggers another procedure.

## 3. DEFINITIONS

**Complaint:** An expression of dissatisfaction about an organisation's product, service, conduct, decision or failure to act where a response or remedy is expected.

**Complainant:** The person or authorised representative raising the complaint.

**Case owner:** The person assigned to coordinate the investigation and response.

**Dispute:** A disagreement about facts, responsibility, contractual interpretation, a transaction, a decision or a proposed remedy.

**Acknowledgement:** Confirmation that the complaint was received and assigned a reference; it is not an admission of liability.

**Resolution:** The recorded outcome, including any remedy, explanation, corrective action or reason no further action is appropriate.

**External escalation:** Referral or submission to a competent regulator, ombud, dispute-resolution body, court or other authorised body, as applicable.

## 4. POLICY PRINCIPLES

All complaints must be handled:
1. fairly, consistently and without retaliation;
2. accessibly, including reasonable assistance for language, disability or communication needs;
3. confidentially and on a need-to-know basis;
4. impartially, with conflicts of interest declared and managed;
5. proportionately to severity, impact, urgency and complexity;
6. with accurate records and preservation of relevant evidence;
7. within approved service targets and any applicable statutory or contractual deadline;
8. without restricting any non-excludable legal right or external remedy.

A complainant must not be required to waive legal rights, accept a settlement or withdraw a complaint as a condition of receiving an acknowledgement or having the matter investigated.

## 5. COMPLAINT SUBMISSION CHANNELS

The organisation must publish only channels that have been verified, staffed and approved for the relevant legal entity and service.

Potential channels include:
- the official website or application complaint form;
- an approved support email address;
- an approved telephone or messaging channel;
- postal correspondence to a verified registered or business address;
- an authorised representative or accessible alternative channel.

The published channel register must state the responsible entity, supported languages, operating hours, accessibility arrangements, expected acknowledgement target, emergency/security reporting route and a tested escalation contact.

Do not insert unverified contact details into this policy or customer-facing pages. If a channel is unavailable, the organisation must provide a reasonable alternative and record the outage.

## 6. INFORMATION TO REQUEST

The organisation should request only information reasonably necessary to understand and investigate the complaint:
- complainant's name and preferred contact method;
- customer, order, invoice or case reference where available;
- relevant product, service or legal entity;
- a clear description of the issue and relevant dates;
- the outcome or remedy sought, if known;
- supporting documents or transaction references, where relevant;
- representative authority, where a representative acts for another person;
- accessibility or communication requirements, if voluntarily provided.

Do not request passwords, one-time passcodes, private keys, seed phrases, full payment-card security codes or unnecessary identity documents. Use approved secure channels for sensitive evidence. A complaint must still be logged if the complainant cannot provide every requested field.

## 7. CASE REGISTRATION AND ACKNOWLEDGEMENT

Every complaint must receive a unique case reference and a timestamp. The case record should include:
- receiving channel and time;
- relevant legal entity, product and service;
- complaint category and severity;
- complainant's preferred communication method;
- assigned case owner and responsible team;
- conflicts or independence concerns;
- evidence references and preservation requirements;
- target dates and any applicable external deadline;
- status, communications, decisions and closure reason.

The acknowledgement should confirm receipt, provide the case reference, explain the next steps and identify how to provide additional evidence. It should not promise an outcome or deadline that has not been approved and verified.

## 8. TRIAGE, PRIORITY AND ROUTING

The case owner must assess urgency, customer impact, potential ongoing harm, financial exposure, data sensitivity, safety implications and any time limit for external escalation.

At minimum, use the following operational classifications:

| Classification | Examples | Required routing |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Immediate safety risk, active account compromise, suspected ongoing fraud, material data/security incident | Immediate referral to the designated incident lead and relevant specialist process |
| High | Material financial impact, widespread service failure, vulnerable customer impact, imminent external deadline | Priority case owner and management visibility |
| Standard | Individual service, billing, product or support complaint | Relevant operational team |
| Complex / disputed | Multiple entities, contested evidence, legal interpretation, repeated unresolved issue | Senior case owner and Legal review |

These are internal triage categories, not statutory classifications. Security, privacy, fraud, safety and regulatory reporting obligations must be assessed independently of the complaint priority.

## 9. INVESTIGATION

The case owner must:
1. define the issues and questions to be resolved;
2. identify relevant systems, records, staff and third parties;
3. preserve original evidence and record its source;
4. verify transaction, account and service status using authoritative records;
5. obtain responses from relevant teams without allowing a conflicted person to determine the outcome;
6. distinguish verified facts, allegations, assumptions and unresolved questions;
7. assess applicable contract terms, published policies and legal requirements;
8. record findings, rationale, reviewer and date.

Evidence may include support correspondence, service logs, order and delivery records, invoices, payment-provider references, approved bank or ledger evidence, consent records, audit logs and relevant technical incident records. Access must be limited to authorised personnel.

No staff member may alter or delete relevant records to influence an investigation. Legal holds and preservation instructions take precedence over routine deletion schedules.

## 10. RESPONSE AND RESOLUTION

The final response should, as appropriate:
- restate the complaint and issues considered;
- summarise relevant verified facts;
- explain the investigation and findings in plain language;
- identify any uncertainty or issue that could not be verified;
- state the decision and reasons;
- describe any remedy, corrective action or next step;
- explain any review or escalation route;
- identify relevant external complaint or dispute options where verified and applicable.

Possible outcomes include:
- complaint upheld in full or in part;
- complaint not upheld, with reasons;
- explanation or clarification provided;
- correction of an error;
- service repair, replacement or re-performance;
- refund, credit or billing correction where authorised and legally appropriate;
- account or security remediation through the relevant controlled process;
- apology or other appropriate service recovery;
- referral to a specialist process;
- no further action, with reasons and review route.

A complaint decision does not itself authorise a payment, refund, bank-detail change, account modification, journal posting or other financial write. Such actions must follow the separate financial approval and execution controls.

## 11. RESPONSE TARGETS AND DEADLINES

Management must approve operational acknowledgement, update and resolution targets for each channel and complaint category.

Before publication, Legal must map applicable statutory, regulatory, ombud, payment-scheme and contractual deadlines for each relevant jurisdiction and product. The shortest applicable mandatory deadline must be observed.

Where an investigation cannot be completed within the applicable target:
- notify the complainant before the target expires where practicable;
- explain the reason for delay;
- provide a revised estimate and next update date;
- identify any interim protection or action;
- record the delay and its approval.

Do not invent a universal statutory response period. Internal targets must never be presented as replacing a legal deadline.

## 12. ESCALATION AND INDEPENDENT REVIEW

A complainant may request review where they disagree with the outcome, identify material new evidence or believe the process was not impartial.

Escalation should be to a person with appropriate authority who was not responsible for the original decision, where practicable.

The escalation record must identify:
- the original decision and date;
- reasons for disagreement;
- new evidence, if any;
- reviewer and independence assessment;
- review findings and rationale;
- revised remedy or confirmation of the original outcome;
- any external route that remains available.

Material, repeated, high-risk or precedent-setting complaints must be referred to Legal and authorised management.

## 13. EXTERNAL DISPUTE RESOLUTION AND REGULATORY REFERRALS

The organisation must not obstruct a complainant from approaching a competent regulator, ombud, consumer authority, privacy authority, payment provider dispute process, court or other external body.

The case owner should identify the relevant external route only after verifying:
- the contracting legal entity;
- the product or service involved;
- the relevant jurisdiction;
- the body's current jurisdiction and eligibility criteria;
- applicable submission requirements and deadlines.

Where a regulator or court makes a lawful request or order, route it promptly to Legal and preserve relevant records. Do not make admissions, waive rights, settle material claims or submit formal legal positions without authorised review.

## 14. FINANCIAL, PAYMENT AND DIGITAL-ASSET COMPLAINTS

Financial complaints require enhanced verification:
- distinguish payment initiation, processing, provider-reported completion, settlement and receipt;
- verify transaction identifiers and source records;
- check for duplicates, reversals, chargebacks, fees, FX and timing differences;
- preserve original currency and amount;
- do not infer ownership or balances from market prices or wallet addresses alone;
- do not describe a payment as received until supported by authoritative settlement evidence.

For stablecoin or blockchain-related complaints, record the network, token contract or mint identifier, transaction hash, block/confirmation evidence, source provider and observation timestamp where available. A transaction hash or explorer display alone may not prove beneficial ownership, custody or legal settlement.

The complaints process must not request or accept seed phrases, private keys, wallet recovery codes or signing credentials. No complaint handler may initiate a transfer, debit order, exchange order, wallet signing or withdrawal as a remedy.

## 15. PRIVACY, CONFIDENTIALITY AND SECURITY

Complaint records may contain personal, financial, identity or security information. The organisation must:
- define and document the lawful purpose and access roles;
- collect only necessary information;
- use approved storage and secure communication channels;
- apply access logging and confidentiality controls;
- assess data-subject requests under the applicable privacy process;
- assess suspected personal-information or security incidents under the incident-response process;
- retain and dispose of records under the approved retention schedule and any legal hold;
- share information with suppliers, advisers or authorities only where authorised and appropriate.

Where an operator or third party processes personal information, the relevant written safeguards and contractual arrangements must be assessed under applicable privacy law.

## 16. VULNERABLE CUSTOMERS AND ACCESSIBILITY

The organisation must consider whether the complainant needs additional support, including accessible formats, interpretation, an authorised representative or a different communication channel.

Staff must avoid unnecessary repetition, unreasonable barriers or pressure to accept an outcome. Any safeguarding concern must be escalated through the relevant specialist procedure.

## 17. CONFLICTS, MISCONDUCT AND RETALIATION

A person who is the subject of a complaint, has a material conflict or may be implicated in the underlying event must not control the investigation or final review.

Retaliation, intimidation, discriminatory treatment or deliberate obstruction of a complainant is prohibited and must be referred to the appropriate management or conduct process.

## 18. RECORD RETENTION AND AUDIT TRAIL

The organisation must retain a proportionate case record, including:
- complaint and acknowledgement;
- case classification and assignment;
- evidence references and preservation actions;
- investigation notes and communications;
- decisions, approvals and remedies;
- escalation and external correspondence;
- closure and customer notification;
- root-cause and corrective-action records.

Retention periods must be set by Legal and Privacy based on applicable law, contractual requirements, limitation periods, business need and data-minimisation principles. This policy does not establish an unverified fixed retention period.

## 19. ROOT-CAUSE ANALYSIS AND MANAGEMENT REPORTING

Management reporting should identify trends without exposing unnecessary personal information. Measures may include:
- complaint volumes by product, channel and category;
- acknowledgement and resolution performance against approved targets;
- overdue and reopened cases;
- upheld and partially upheld complaints;
- recurring causes and affected services;
- financial impact and remedy categories;
- security, privacy and fraud referrals;
- external escalations and outcomes;
- corrective actions, owners and completion status.

Recurring or systemic issues must be assigned a corrective-action owner, target date and verification evidence. Closing an individual complaint does not close a systemic risk.

## 20. COMPLAINTS SYSTEM AND PAGE REQUIREMENTS

The customer-facing and internal system should provide, where applicable:
1. Public complaint information page.
2. Accessible complaint submission form.
3. Secure evidence upload.
4. Case reference generation.
5. Acknowledgement and status notifications.
6. Internal triage and assignment queue.
7. Investigation and evidence workspace.
8. Escalation and independent-review workflow.
9. Resolution and remedy record.
10. External referral information.
11. Management dashboard and trend reporting.
12. Audit, access and retention controls.

The interface must not imply that submission guarantees a refund, legal outcome or immediate response. Do not publish a channel until it has been tested and assigned to an accountable owner.

## 21. RESPONSIBILITIES

**Corporate Governance:** Policy ownership and oversight.

**Customer Support / Complaints Officer:** Intake, acknowledgement, case coordination and customer updates.

**Business Owner:** Investigation of product and service issues and delivery of approved corrective action.

**Finance:** Verification of billing, payment and settlement evidence; execution of approved financial remedies through the separate finance process.

**Legal Counsel:** Legal interpretation, material disputes, external proceedings and jurisdiction-specific requirements.

**Privacy / Information Officer:** Privacy requests, personal-information handling and relevant incident assessment.

**Information Security:** Security incidents, account compromise and evidence preservation.

**Authorised Management:** Approval of material settlements, exceptions and systemic remediation under the Delegation of Authority.

## 22. EXCEPTIONS

Any exception must be documented with its rationale, scope, risk, compensating controls, owner, expiry date and authorised approval. No exception may waive a mandatory legal obligation, prevent access to an external remedy or permit retaliation.
